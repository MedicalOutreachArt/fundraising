TJ Rameli

5 works in the collection

TJ Rameli is a visual artist born in Kuala Terengganu and currently based in Selangor, Malaysia. Spending a large portion of his life crafting music and visual arts, mostly for independent music circles, while plying his trade in a few small advertising agencies doing graphic design and art directing. After deciding not to continue with agency life, he picked up his tools again and began his lifelong artistic habits by drawing human figuratives exploring motions and emotions, focusing on personal and social matters. He is currently working independently, with his works residing in a number of private collections. Artist statement: “My work explores human figures as moving vessels of emotion within a landscape of memory. Through my paintings and drawings, I capture the subtle tension between presence and absence, the visible and the unseen. I approach figurative painting not as a mirror of reality but as a process of distillation, where forms always shift between recognition and abstraction. Each brushstroke is guided by my instinct as much as observation, allowing chance and intuition to shape the figures as they emerge. In doing so, I invite viewers to recognize themselves in fragments—gestures, silences, traces of feeling—a constant motion that keeps reminding us that a figure is never just one body, but a shared space of human connection.”

Chong Siew Ying

1 work in the collection

Chong Siew Ying (b.1969, Kuala Lumpur) trained in France at the École des Beaux-Arts de Versaillais and Atelier 63, spending nearly two decades in Paris before returning to Malaysia. Chong was also awarded the Special Prize at Malaysian Young Contemporary Artists Competition (2002) and was a finalist for the Sovereign Asian Art Prize, Hong Kong (2009). Her work appears in major private collections as well as at Kuala Lumpur National Art Gallery and the Singapore Art Museum.

SC Shekar

1 work in the collection

SC Shekar is a Malaysian photojournalist, documentary photographer, filmmaker and publisher whose career spans more than three and a half decades. From the streets of New York to the riverbanks of Varanasi and the interiors of Southeast Asia, his work is rooted in patient, honest visual storytelling. He began in the early 1980s as a photo-journalist working for newspapers and news agencies around the world, honing the instincts of a visual journalist: to observe closely, respond quickly and tell stories with clarity and integrity. His practice later evolved into long-form documentary projects that explore people, place, memory and change. Shekar has published more than 29 books, many of them large-format works recognised for artistic strength, cultural value and historical importance. Notable among them is Grit & Grace – The Grandeur of Monochrome Malaysia, a landmark book of aerial images of Peninsular Malaysia, Sabah and Sarawak. Central to his work is a long-standing commitment to Malaysia’s Indigenous communities. For over two decades, he has documented the lives and struggles of the Orang Asli and Indigenous peoples in Sabah and Sarawak, building trust and long-term relationships while using his exhibitions, books and talks to raise awareness and support. He is the founder of SC Shekar Photography Sdn Bhd and Beagle Books Sdn Bhd.

Chua Chong Sheng

1 work in the collection

Chua Chong Sheng, born in 2005 in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, began his art journey in late 2023 during what was supposed to be his gap year after completing high school. That first trial art class led him on an unconventional pathway where his creativity was unearthed and highlighted in his paintings of whimsical houses, holiday-inspired nature paintings, and works that reflect his love for cars. From having the odds stacked against him — born premature at 32 weeks with a grim prognosis, in the NICU at the age of 1 month, unlikely to be able to attend school — to who he is today, his life journey, which began with multiple hospitalisations and continues with the multi-faceted challenges brought on by his cerebral palsy, is a quiet yet powerful underlying message embedded in all his paintings: you need not be limited by a label. Using his usual medium of acrylic paint, acrylic markers and pigment ink on canvas, his cheerful and vibrant paintings have been shared with the public at group exhibitions including Sport My Art (2024), Telur Pecah 4.0 (2024), Discovering A Beautiful Mind (2025), and Beautiful Malaysia (2026), and via his Instagram account @artbychongsheng. His paintings, commissioned artworks, and merchandise have found their way to new homes in Malaysia, Singapore and the UK, spreading beauty, joy and hope to all.

Chung Xin Kai

2 works in the collection

Xin Kai was born in 2003 in Subang Jaya, Selangor, Malaysia. He is an artist who uses drawing and painting to share his thoughts and feelings. The art he creates is inspired by what he is interested and cares about, turning personal emotions into images that help him communicate — especially when expressing through words is difficult. At the age of 4, Xin Kai was diagnosed with a learning deficiency. After his diagnosis, he was introduced to various lessons to improve his overall wellbeing, such as piano playing and swimming. Everything eventually changed when his parents discovered his incredible drawing abilities when he was 7 years old. Hence, they started enrolling him into drawing lessons. Since then, Xin Kai's art journey has officially begun. Over the years, Xin Kai has developed a strong love for drawing. What started as a personal journey has grown into a lasting passion. Creating art brings joy, comfort, and peace. Xin Kai hopes that through his art, people can feel these emotions too. By turning inner ideas into visible artworks, Xin Kai works to bring clarity and life to his imagination, making sure each piece of art truly reflects his feelings, wishes, and growing artistic voice.

Janet Lee (1989-2023)

2 works in the collection

Janet Lee (1989–2023) was a Malaysian colourist celebrated for her vibrant, intuitive use of colour. Based in Kuala Lumpur, she worked across acrylics, pastels, watercolours, coloured pencils, marker pens, and Chinese ink. Her bold and pastel tones, paired with carefree, fluid strokes, conveyed spontaneity and emotional clarity. Her artistic journey began with simple wave-like patterns in childhood and evolved following formal training in 2010. Over time, her work expanded into landscapes, florals, and notable series such as Time of Day and Balloons, where rhythm, repetition, and colour became central to her expression. Janet lived with epilepsy from infancy and was later diagnosed with an intellectual disability, yet remained deeply committed to her art throughout her life. She continued creating until her passing in 2023. Her works have been exhibited both locally and internationally, including in The Hidden Truths Project (USA). Her artworks are held in private collections worldwide and by the Korea Culture Art Research Institute in Seoul. Today, her legacy continues through the Janet Lee Gallery — sharing her art, supporting neurodiverse creatives, and expanding conversations around inclusion through art, storytelling, and community engagement.

Mohd Azlan MAM

1 work in the collection

Mohd Azlan MAM (b. 1974, Balik Pulau, Penang) is a Malaysian contemporary visual artist whose practice intersects medical imaging, archival aesthetics, and socio-political critique. Trained in diagnostic imaging, he employs collage, scanography, analog photography, risograph printmaking, hand-tinting, and found historical materials to construct layered visual investigations into memory, nationalism, religion, race, and institutional power. His works are distinguished by faceless figures, fractured archives, and dense metaphorical imagery that challenge official narratives. Major series include The Ballads of Local Magnitude, The Memoirs of Panopticon Brotherhood, and The Mapping of Wayang Kertas. He lives and works in Kuala Lumpur while continuing a professional career in advanced cardiovascular imaging. Artist statement: "My practice investigates the epistemology of the image — how visual culture constructs, mediates, and contests knowledge rather than merely representing reality. Drawing upon more than three decades of professional experience in diagnostic and cardiovascular imaging, I approach artistic production as a form of visual investigation in which images function as instruments of inquiry rather than objects of aesthetic consumption... I develop what I describe as a Tomographic Visual Philosophy — an epistemological methodology that borrows conceptually from medical imaging. Like tomography, my works seek to reconstruct hidden structures that remain invisible beneath the surface of social and political life... my work asks not simply what we see, but how we have learned to see." — Azlan MAM

Diagnosed with autism and facing slow learning challenges, Yi Shen found his voice through art. Shafiq Nazri

2 works in the collection

Shafiq Nazri was born in 1992 in Shah Alam, Selangor. Born prematurely at 900 grams, he developed Hyaline Membrane Disease and was placed on a ventilator for 12 weeks. He subsequently developed Retrolental Fibroplasia, which left him with complete retinal detachment in the right eye and impaired vision in the left. Serial hearing evaluations found severe neurosensory deafness from about 6 months of age, and he has no comprehensive speech. Shafiq is profoundly deaf, partially blind, hyperactive, and was diagnosed as autistic at the age of 10. At age 15 he experienced his first seizure and has since been diagnosed as epileptic, requiring lifelong medication. His learning capacity is very limited. He attends Pusat Jagaan Sayang, where he loves to paint — abstract painting is his way of expressing himself. As a hyperactive child he cannot sit still for long, so each painting takes time to complete, depending on his mood. Despite all the medical issues he faces, he continues to paint, occasionally but persistently.

Ainaa Farhanah (Conteng by Ainaa)

Ainaa Farhanah is a Malaysian visual artist and the founder of Conteng by Ainaa. She holds a degree in Graphic Design from Nottingham Trent University, completed through a twinning programme with First City University, and has participated in over 30 events, exhibitions, and competitions, reflecting an active and evolving professional practice. Living with Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA), Ainaa approaches art as both creative expression and personal resilience. Each artwork is allowed to evolve naturally, embracing the unpredictability of the hand-drawn mark. At its core, Ainaa's work explores themes of impermanence and the human touch within an increasingly standardised world.

MS

Master Sum Kwai Fong

2 works in the collection

Master Sum Kwai Fong (1948–2024) was a distinguished Malaysian Chinese calligraphy & brush painting artist. He dedicated his life to preserving and promoting the beauty of traditional Chinese art. A graduate of the Malaysian Institute of Art (MIA), Master Sum began his teaching career in 1975 and spent his life mentoring and inspiring students. He is recognised as one of the world's top 100 Chinese calligraphy and brush painting artists. Master Sum was also a founding member and past president of the 1 Malaysia Artists Association, where he made significant contributions to the growth and appreciation of Chinese art in Malaysia. Throughout his career he held many exhibitions and charity functions, and his paintings were acquired by collectors and art enthusiasts. Master Sum once said in an interview: "The creation of art in painting is not only a painting on the surface, nor is it an objective copy of the picture of life. Instead, it is the artist's understanding of life, attitude towards social things, and the expression of his own thoughts and personality."
